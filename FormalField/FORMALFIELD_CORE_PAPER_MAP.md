# FormalField Core: A Lean-Certified Structural Readout Kernel

## 1. Paper Thesis

This paper introduces a Lean-certified structural kernel for organizing carrier, metric, residual, gate, cost, readout, verification, classification, and terminal closure inside a single trusted formal system.

The scope is structural. The paper presents a proof kernel for disciplined readout and closure, not a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, a Theory of Everything, an RSA collapse, or any physics claim.

## 2. Central Contribution

FormalField Core L1–L15 provides a trusted formal skeleton for:

- defining structural processes,
- separating structural results from interpretive results,
- preventing closure before verification,
- distinguishing assumption from closure,
- preventing circular closure,
- classifying terminal outputs.

The contribution is therefore a formal architecture for trusted structural reasoning, rather than a solved external application theorem.

## 6. Main Structural Theorem Candidate

## 10. Suggested Abstract

FormalField Core is a Lean-certified structural readout kernel for organizing carrier formation, metric structure, residual control, gate and cost discipline, readout admissibility, verification, classification, and terminal closure inside a trusted formal system. The project isolates structural reasoning from interpretive claims by requiring outputs to pass through explicit verification and classification layers before they may count as closed results. It further separates assumption from closure, excludes circular closure, and formalizes terminal status distinctions between closed result, accumulation, rejected readout, and pending integration. The resulting kernel is intended as a reusable proof architecture rather than an application-specific theorem. To preserve that boundary, external domains such as RH, TOE, RSA, and physics are represented only as obligation shells above the frozen core, each with explicit structural requirements and explicit unresolved obligations. The paper therefore contributes a disciplined formal kernel for structural readout and closure, while making an equally explicit non-claim: it does not solve external mathematical, physical, or cryptographic problems merely by formalizing the kernel on which future application branches may depend.
